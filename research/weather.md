# Weather — northern Vietnam, late December → early January

Researched 2026-10-07. Window under study: **~24 Dec 2026 → ~2 Jan 2027**.

## The one-line version
The north in this window is **cool, dry and misty, not rainy** — the wettest months are
long gone. The two things that decide whether a day is good are **fog/visibility** and
**cold on the high passes**. Ninh Binh and Hanoi are reliably pleasant. Ha Giang town is
cool and often misty, and its high passes (Dong Van, Ma Pi Leng, ~1,000–1,400 m) are
genuinely cold with a real chance of morning fog and, rarely, ice. Sapa/Fansipan and
Ha Long/Cat Ba are the weak spots — cold, fogged in, and (for Ha Long) with a real risk
of cancelled sailings.

## Region by region

### Hanoi — GOOD
- **December:** daily highs ~15–23°C, lows ~12–16°C; **cool, misty, atmospheric, and one
  of the driest months.** Late Dec through Jan gets colder — the official Vietnam
  tourism site says from late December to March lows reach **10°C**.
  Sources: Vietnam Airlines travel guide "Weather in Hanoi in December"
  (https://www.vietnamairlines.com/at/en/plan-book/travel/travel-guide/weather-in-hanoi-vietnam-in-december,
  checked 2026-10-07); https://vietnam.travel/things-to-do/weather-and-climate-vietnam
  (checked 2026-10-07); Weatherspark December normals for Hanoi
  (https://weatherspark.com/m/116009/12/Average-Weather-in-December-in-Hanoi-Vietnam)
- **What it feels like:** a damp cold, not a crisp cold. You want a jacket in the evening
  and a light layer by day. Overcast and light mist are common; genuinely heavy rain is
  not. **Photography:** mist + low winter sun makes the Old Quarter lovely; expect grey
  skies more often than blue ones. Hanoi's air pollution is also worst in winter — MFA
  specifically warns the PM10 dust index in Hanoi can run ~4× international recommended
  levels. Source: MFA Vietnam page, checked 2026-10-07.

### Ninh Binh — GOOD (the best weather of the three bases)
- December: **~16–22°C**, low rainfall; some sources put the range at 17–22°C daytime.
  Sources: https://www.tnktravel.com/blogs/ninh-binh-vietnam-december-weather/ and
  https://vietnamtravel.com/vietnam-weather-in-december/ (both checked 2026-10-07);
  AccuWeather's December outlook for Ninh Binh lists highs 64–76°F (18–24°C), lows
  56–68°F (13–20°C). All checked 2026-10-07.
- **What it feels like:** the same cool, damp cold as Hanoi but two hours further south
  and out in open countryside, so it feels fresher. Mornings can be misty over the karst
  — which is exactly when Trang An and Tam Coc look best. Expect **drizzle rather than
  downpours**; boats run in the rain (they're covered, though the rower is not).
- **Mud:** cycling the back lanes after rain means real paddy-field mud. Bring shoes you
  don't mind.
- **Photography:** this is the strongest bet of the three bases. Hang Mua sunrise over
  Tam Coc, the Trang An river caves in mist, the Bich Dong pagoda cut into the cliff.

### Ha Giang (Ha Giang city, Quan Ba, Yen Minh, Dong Van, Meo Vac) — GOOD, with caveats
- **December: winter returns.** Cold, often misty, cloud on the high passes; visibility
  can change quickly and daylight is short. Exposed passes feel *much* colder than the
  towns because of wind. January–February is colder and mistier still.
  Source: https://skypathhagiang.com/ha-giang-weather-by-month/ (operator guide,
  checked 2026-10-07) — treat as **operator guidance**.
- **Better news:** December is the *dry* end of the year — the wet season is June–August.
  Late December is often recommended precisely because the air is clearer than in the
  burning/smoky months and roads are dry. Operator report of 4 Dec 2025 gives daytime
  **16–20°C** on the loop with light wind and no rain
  (https://www.instagram.com/reel/DR07GQuE2Fy/ — social post, **lead not fact**).
- **What it feels like:** Ha Giang city (elev. ~120 m) is mild-ish. **Dong Van old town
  (~1,000–1,400 m) is properly cold** — think a Hanoi January night, with wind on a
  moving motorbike. On an easy-rider pillion you are *not* generating heat: a windproof
  shell matters more than a thick jumper.
- **Fog and ice:** the standard advice from operators is **start mid-morning** to let
  dense morning fog and any icy patches clear
  (https://jasminehagiang.com/news/ha-giang-loop-weather-december-to-january-ultimate-guide-n126.html,
  checked 2026-10-07 — operator blog, **lead**). Riders report highs around 13°C with
  cloudy/rainy days still rideable (Reddit r/solotravel thread
  https://www.reddit.com/r/solotravel/comments/zuut49/ — **anecdotal**).
- **Verdict:** the loop is doable and worth doing in this window; the risk is *not*
  rain, it's one fogged-in day where Ma Pi Leng shows you nothing. Budget the loop at
  3 days so a bad first morning doesn't cost you the whole highlight.

### Sapa / Fansipan — BAD BET (excluded)
- Winter runs Dec–Feb with **average daytime ~7°C, night-time 0°C or below**, and
  "thick fog often covers the" mountain. Source: Vietnam Airlines, "Best time to visit
  Fansipan" (https://www.vietnamairlines.com/go/en/plan-book/travel/travel-guide/best-time-to-visit-fansipan,
  checked 2026-10-07).
- **Frost is common; snow is rare but does happen** in strong cold snaps, especially at
  altitude. Snowfall on Fansipan "relatively rare and doesn't happen every year".
  Sources: https://www.tnktravel.com/blogs/sapa-vietnam-december-weather/ and
  https://www.asiatouradvisor.com/get-inspired/vietnam/does-it-snow-in-vietnam/
  (both checked 2026-10-07).
- **Why we're skipping it:** trekking is cold, muddy and often fogged out; Fansipan's
  summit cable car in fog is a very expensive white-out; and it duplicates the Ha Giang
  scenery with a worse weather profile. Same season, worse odds.

### Cat Ba / Ha Long Bay — BAD BET (excluded)
- December: foggy, cold; highs ~21°C, lows ~15°C. January averages ~17°C with early
  morning fog. Sources: https://www.halongbaytours.com/trip-planning/weather/halong-bay/december.html
  and https://www.cruisehalongbay.com/blog/ha-long-bay-weather-what-month-should-you-travel-to-ha-long-and-what-is-the-most-beautiful-season
  (both checked 2026-10-07 — **commercial cruise/tour sites, treat as leads**).
- One cruise operator makes the point that most winter departures still run
  (https://bhayacruises.com/blog/halong-bay-weather-and-best-time-visit/), but another
  states plainly that **fog, monsoon and strong winter wind are among the reasons
  cruises are cancelled** (https://www.indochina-junk.com/ha-long-bay-weather/). Both
  checked 2026-10-07.
- **Why we're skipping it:** you pay for 1–2 nights on a boat to look at limestones
  through grey fog, and there is a non-trivial chance of a cancellation that wrecks two
  days of an eight-day trip. Ha Giang gives you the same karst drama with a clearer
  reason to be there.

### Mai Chau / Pu Luong — VIABLE BUT NOT NEEDED
- Pu Luong in December: **~15–22°C**, serene and cool, rice harvested so the terraces are
  gold rather than green — i.e. you are seeing the post-harvest version of the postcard.
  Sources: Facebook travel-group post (https://www.facebook.com/groups/vietnamtravelguideofficial/posts/903861048596093/,
  **lead**) and http://www.vietnammountainstay.com/best-time-to-visit-pu-luong-a-season-by-season-guide-to-pu-luong-weather/
  (checked 2026-10-07). Nights 10–12°C, days 14–18°C in the cold stretch.
- **Verdict:** genuinely the closest thing to a "warmer, easier Ha Giang" and good for
  homestays — but it delivers less drama per day than Ha Giang and it's another long
  transfer from Hanoi (~3.5–4 h to Mai Chau, more to Pu Luong). Excluded on the
  "max 2–3 bases" rule, not on weather.

### Cao Bang / Ban Gioc waterfall — BAD BET (excluded)
- Cao Bang winter is **cold and foggy, 10–18°C by day, down to ~5°C at night**, with
  little rain. Sources: https://www.handspan.com/en/time-to-visit-cao-bang.html and
  travelvietnam.com (both checked 2026-10-07 — **commercial, leads**).
- **Ban Gioc is at its weakest in winter** — this is the dry season, so flow is at its
  lowest, and the surrounding route is fog-prone.
- **Why we're skipping it:** Cao Bang is a 5-day loop in its own right (Ha Giang
  operators sell a combined Ha Giang + Cao Bang 5D4N at 7,800,000 VND — see
  `transport.md`). There is no version of that which fits an 8–9 day trip that also
  includes Hanoi and Ninh Binh.

## Regions that are a bad bet in this window — plainly
1. **Sapa / Fansipan** — 7°C days, fog, frost, mud, out-of-season terraces.
2. **Cat Ba / Ha Long Bay** — fog, cold, cancellation risk on the very thing you paid for.
3. **Cao Bang / Ban Gioc** — too far, waterfall at dry-season low.
Everything else is fine. Ninh Binh is the best weather of the candidates; Ha Giang is
the best *scenery* and costs you a fog-risk day or two.

## What this means for photography
- **Fog is your friend, grey skies are not.** In Ninh Binh and Ha Giang, target
  **sunrise** — the mist sits in the valleys and the karst peaks poke out of it. By
  mid-morning it's usually burnt off to flat grey.
- **Rice terraces are out of season.** Harvest is over by December in the north; Ha Giang
  and Pu Luong terraces will be brown/gold or bare, and the buckwheat flower season
  (Ha Giang's big one) is **October–November**, already finished. Do not expect the
  pink-and-green postcard. What you *do* get is **better roads, less haze and fewer
  people than the Sept–Nov peak**.
- **Plan around the light, not the weather app.** In December the sun sets around
  17:15–17:30 in the north, so a "golden hour" landscape shot means being in position by
  about 16:15.
- **Hanoi's winter light is soft and misty** — good for street photography, bad for wide
  blue-sky shots. Train Street, Long Bien Bridge and the Old Quarter all work in flat
  light.

## Season notes and holidays
- **Late December is peak tourist season** in northern Vietnam (cool + dry = everyone
  comes), and 24 Dec – 2 Jan is the peak *of* the peak for Hanoi hotel prices.
- **Tet (Lunar New Year) 2027 falls on 6 February 2027** — the Vietnamese public holiday
  period runs into mid-February. Sources: Wikipedia "Tết" (2027 date: 6 February) and
  https://www.themystdongkhoihotel.com/tet-2027-in-saigon (both checked 2026-10-07).
  **This does not affect the trip.** It does mean: do not drift the dates into February.
- **Public holidays in the window:** 1 January (New Year's Day) is a public holiday in
  Vietnam — banks and some government offices close; shops and restaurants in tourist
  areas stay open. No other holiday falls inside 24 Dec – 2 Jan. (Not verified against an
  official Vietnamese holiday list; treat as standard knowledge, not a sourced claim.)

## Caveats
- All temperature ranges above are **climatological normals, not a forecast for Dec 2026**.
  No forecast for that window exists yet (it is 2.5 months away). Re-check ~7 days out.
- Ha Giang and Cao Bang figures come substantially from **operator blogs and travel
  sites**, not meteorological services. The Hanoi and Ninh Binh figures are corroborated
  by Vietnam Airlines' own travel guide, vietnam.travel (official tourism) and
  Weatherspark/AccuWeather.
- The Vietnam National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting
  (https://kttv.gov.vn/) is the authoritative source for re-checking before departure.

# Food & experiences — per region

Researched 2026-10-07. Named places are sourced; where I could only source a *dish* and
not a *venue*, I say so rather than inventing one.
Prices: 1 SGD = 20,285 VND (open.er-api.com, 6 Oct 2026).

## HANOI

### Signature dishes
**Phở** (beef or chicken noodle soup, eaten at breakfast — the broth is the whole point) ·
**bún chả** (charcoal-grilled pork patties + pork belly, cold rice vermicelli, nước chấm
dipping sauce — Hanoi's own invention) · **chả cá** (turmeric-marinated grilled catfish
with dill and spring onion, fried at the table) · **bánh mì** · **bún riêu** (tangy
tomato-crab vermicelli) · **miến lươn** (eel vermicelli) · **cà phê trứng** (egg coffee —
Hanoi's own, whipped egg yolk over strong coffee).

### Coffee pick
**Cà phê trứng (egg coffee)** — Hanoi's invention. The most-cited original is
**Giang Café** on Hàng Gai Street: cramped, cash-only, queues, worth it
(https://wanderinvietnam.com/guides/famous-food-in-hanoi-what-to-buy-taste-avoid-in-2026,
checked 2026-10-07 — **blog, a lead**). If you'd rather have it with a proper breakfast,
**Habakuk** (4 Phan Huy Chú Lane, Bib Gourmand) is a specialty coffee shop by day and a
bistro by night.
*The egg coffee itself is identical at 25,000 and at 80,000 VND; you're paying for the room.*

### Underrated
**Lunch on Hai Bà Trưng near Hàng Trọng.** Locals eat there, tourists haven't found it,
fewer stalls means less crowding and bún chả / rice bowls are strong
(wanderinvietnam.com, checked 2026-10-07). Also: go to **Đồng Xuân market at 5–9 a.m.**
rather than at tourist hours.

### Experiences & photography
- **Train Street** — the alley where a mainline train passes inches from the cafés. **You
  cannot simply walk in any more; police block the entrances**, and since 25 Mar tourists
  are not allowed to freely enter the trackside cafés — the working method is **to sit in a
  café as a paying customer and watch the train from there**, and to check the day's train
  times locally. Sources: https://chrisandwrensworld.com/hanoi-train-street/ and Reddit
  r/hanoi reports (both checked 2026-10-07 — **blog/community, leads**). Expect to be
  turned away; have a backup.
- **Long Biên Bridge** — the rusted, century-old cantilever bridge. Sunset from the
  mid-span walkway is the classic Hanoi photograph.
- **Hoàn Kiếm Lake at dusk** — Turtle Tower, the red Húc Bridge to Ngọc Sơn temple.
- **St Joseph's Cathedral** — neo-Gothic, and the square in front is a hangout.
- **Temple of Literature** (Văn Miếu) — Vietnam's first university. Entrance fee
  **could not confirm**.
- **Hỏa Lò Prison** — "the Hanoi Hilton". Sources conflict on the ticket: 30,000 vs
  50,000 VND (checked 2026-10-07); the night tour is a separate ticket.
- **Old Quarter food crawl + "beer corner"** at Tạ Hiện — bia hoi at 10,000–15,000 VND a
  glass (daytripsvietnam.com Bia Hoi Index 2026, checked 2026-10-07).

### Christmas / New Year specific
Hanoi's **New Year's Eve fireworks are over Hoàn Kiếm Lake**, with stages near the
**Opera House** and at **Thống Nhất Park**; crowds gather from the Húc Bridge area.
Source: https://www.getyourguide.com/explorer/hanoi-ttd205/new-years-eve-in-hanoi/ (checked
2026-10-07 — **commercial, corroborated by local reports and Reddit r/hanoi**).
**Christmas Day itself is a normal working day in Hanoi** — Vietnam is not a Christian
country, so expect fairy lights and a full Old Quarter rather than closures. **I could not
find a sourced schedule for church services at St Joseph's Cathedral for 25 Dec 2026.**
**1 January is a public holiday** — banks and government offices close; tourist
restaurants and shops stay open.

## NINH BINH

### Signature dishes
**Thịt dê (mountain goat)** — grilled, stir-fried, steamed, or in a hotpot; the local
speciality, and the reason to eat here · **cơm cháy** (crispy burnt rice, often served with
goat) · **miến lươn** (eel vermicelli) · **ốc núi** (mountain snails) · **cơm chay** — the
vegetarian rice dish associated with the region's pagodas.

### Named places
- **Father Cooking Restaurant** — Tam Coc area (Ninh Hải, Hoa Lư). ~796 TripAdvisor
  reviews, consistently recommended for **goat meat**.
  https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowUserReviews-g12866457-d6780989 (checked 2026-10-07 —
  **review site, lead**).
- The tourist offices and guides point to clusters of goat-meat restaurants around
  **Tam Coc, Trang An and Ninh Binh city**
  (https://ninhbinhtouristcenter.com/where-to-taste-ninh-binhs-signature-goat-meat-and-local-cuisine/,
  checked 2026-10-07 — **commercial, lead**).
- **Caveat:** Ninh Binh's named-restaurant coverage online is dominated by SEO farm sites.
  I could not independently verify a second tier of named places. **Ask your homestay** —
  in Tam Coc that will beat anything I can source from here.

### Coffee pick
Tam Coc's cafés are mostly attached to homestays and look over the paddies — pick one with
a rice-field view at sunset. **No specific café could be sourced by name.**

### Underrated
**Rent a bicycle (50,000 VND/day from most Tam Coc homestays) and ride the lanes out to
Bích Động and the Thái Vi temple** rather than doing everything by boat. This is the
postcard Ninh Binh and almost nobody who does the day-trip-from-Hanoi version sees it,
because they arrive at 10 a.m. and leave at 4 p.m.

### Experiences & photography
- **Trang An boat trip** — 250,000 VND, ~2.5–3 h, three routes, UNESCO World Heritage;
  limestone caves and riverside temples. **The longer, better boat.**
- **Hang Mua ("Mua Cave")** — ~500 stone steps to the dragon-statue viewpoint over Tam
  Coc. **The single best photograph in Ninh Binh, and best at sunrise** — the tour buses
  arrive from Hanoi at 9–10 a.m., boats start at 7–8 a.m., so that two-hour gap is yours.
  Fee quoted between 100,000 and 200,000 VND across sources.
- **Tam Coc boat** — 150,000–200,000 VND, ~1.5–2 h, three caves, foot-paddled; rowers
  expect a tip on top of the ticket.
- **Bích Động pagoda** — three temples cut into the cliff, staircase between them.
- **Hoa Lư** — the 10th-century capital; temples and citadel walls.
- **Sunset over the Tam Coc paddies** from a homestay roof.

## HA GIANG (and the loop)

### Signature dishes
**Thắng cố** (thang co) — the H'Mong market stew, traditionally horse meat simmered with
herbs and offal; now often beef or buffalo. **It is an acquired taste and a real cultural
set-piece** — try it at a market, not at a restaurant. · **Cháo ấu tẩu** (au tau
porridge) — a rice porridge made with a rare, slightly bitter mountain root, sold as a
warming breakfast on cold mornings. · **Bánh tam giác mạch** (buckwheat cake) — the
buckwheat-flour griddle cake associated with the plateau. · **Cơm lam** (rice cooked in
bamboo) · **five-colour sticky rice** · **smoked buffalo meat** · **mèn mén** (steamed
cornmeal).
Sources: https://vietnam.travel/things-to-do/local-unpacked-one-kind-cuisine-ha-giang-karst-plateau
(official Vietnam Tourism, checked 2026-10-07) and
https://www.handspan.com/en/foods-in-ha-giang.html (operator, checked 2026-10-07).

### Named places
**Honest answer: there are no reliably-sourced named restaurants for the loop.**
Coverage online is entirely operator marketing, and the good food is at **village markets
and homestays**, not in restaurants. What I can point to:
- **Đồng Văn market** — the plateau's main market; the Sunday market is the set-piece.
  Thắng cố and market food. (vietnam.travel, checked 2026-10-07.)
- **Your homestay dinner.** On the loop, homestay meals are family-style and included —
  smoked buffalo, sticky rice, mountain vegetables. This is the meal, not a restaurant.
- **Ha Giang city** has ordinary Vietnamese restaurants around Nguyễn Trãi Street near the
  hotels. Nothing I can name from a verifiable source.

### Coffee pick
Coffee on the loop is homestay-and-roadside-stall standard — strong, sweet, condensed milk.
**The view is the product.** No specific café could be sourced by name.

### Underrated
**Ấu tẩu porridge (cháo ấu tẩu) at dawn on a cold pass morning.** It's the local
hangover-and-hypothermia cure, it's cheap, and almost no visitor orders it because the
name is unpronounceable. Bitter, warming, unforgettable.

### Experiences & photography (the loop, in route order)
- **Quản Bạ "Heaven's Gate"** (Cổng trời Quản Bạ) — the twin mountains viewpoint at the
  top of the first big climb; the loop's opening statement.
- **Thẩm Mã Pass** — the famous switchback shelf road.
- **Đồng Văn old town** — old H'Mong houses, the market, the H'Mong King's Palace
  (dinh Vua Mèo) nearby.
- **Mã Pì Lèng Pass** — **the highlight of the trip.** 20 km of road cut into a cliff above
  the Nho Quế river gorge; the single best ride and the single best photograph. The viewing
  platform is the classic frame.
- **Nho Quế river boat trip** — down in the gorge below Mã Pì Lèng; often included in tour
  prices.
- **Tù Sản canyon** — seen from Mã Pì Lèng; among the deepest in Southeast Asia.
- **Lũng Cú flag tower** — the northernmost point of Vietnam, near Lô Lô Chải village.
- **Lũng Khùy cave** — limestone cave near Đồng Văn, less visited.
- **Dữ Già village + waterfalls** — the reason to do a **4-day** loop instead of 3: the
  quiet southern leg, with waterfall swims and a village homestay.
- **Sunday markets** (Đồng Văn, Mèo Vạc, Yên Minh) — livestock, textiles, thắng cố, and the
  plateau's social hub. **Timing matters: these are weekly, so checking which village's
  market falls on your day is worth doing before you book the departure day.**

### Practical food notes for the loop
- **Tell your operator about any dietary requirement now, not on the mountain.** Vegetarian
  food is genuinely hard on the loop.
- **Carry cash.** There are ATMs in Hà Giang city and Đồng Văn; between them, nothing.
- **Everything is cheap:** local meals run 40,000–80,000 VND (S$2–4). Village homestays
  often include dinner and breakfast in a 100,000–200,000 VND rate.
  (https://wherebest.com/en/ha-giang-trip-budget and
  https://wanderinvietnam.com/guides/ha-giang-on-a-budget-real-costs-how-to-save, both
  checked 2026-10-07.)
- **December is cold at altitude** — the food you'll want is the hot, bitter, fatty kind.
  Good news: that's exactly what the plateau cooks.

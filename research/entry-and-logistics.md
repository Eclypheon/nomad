# Entry & logistics — Singapore passport holders, Vietnam, Dec 2026 / Jan 2027

Researched 2026-10-07 (Singapore). All "checked" dates below are 2026-10-07 unless stated.

## HEADLINE: no visa needed. Do not buy one.

Singapore ordinary passport holders enter Vietnam **visa-free**. Vietnam's own government
portal lists Singapore as having a bilateral visa-exemption agreement covering
*ordinary* passports; Singapore MFA puts the permitted stay at **up to 30 days per entry**.

- Vietnam Government Portal, "Visa exemption", exemption list row 44 (Singapore,
  agreements dated 10/5/1997 and 01/12/2003): *"Ordinary Passports with valid periods of
  at least 6 months, together with return air tickets or air tickets for leaving the
  country."* — https://vietnam.gov.vn/visa-exemption-68946 (checked 2026-10-07)
- Singapore MFA, Vietnam travel page: *"Generally, Singapore Passport Holders do not
  require a visa to enter Vietnam and can remain up to 30 days per entry."* —
  https://www.mfa.gov.sg/travelling-overseas/travel-advisories-notices-and-visa-information/vietnam/
  (checked 2026-10-07)

An 8–9 day trip is comfortably inside 30 days. **Kester does not need an e-visa, a
visa-on-arrival letter, or any agent's "approval letter".**

### The 45-day exemption is NOT the one that applies here
Vietnam raised the *unilateral* visa-free stay from 15 to 45 days for 13 countries
(Germany, France, Italy, Spain, UK, Russia, Japan, South Korea, Denmark, Sweden, Norway,
Finland, Belarus) effective 15 August 2023. Singapore is not on that list; Singapore's
entry is under the separate bilateral agreement (30 days).
Source: https://vietnam.travel/things-to-do/13-coutries-visa-exemption-can-stay-vietnam-45-days (checked 2026-10-07)

If you see "45 days for Singapore" on a blog or a Facebook group, it is wrong for a
Singapore passport. Plan on 30.

### If anyone in the group is NOT on a Singapore passport
Then the answer changes. Vietnam's e-visa is issued to most nationalities, valid **up to
90 days, single or multiple entry**, and is applied for at the official portal. Fee:
**US$25 single-entry / US$50 multiple-entry**.
- Official portal: https://evisa.gov.vn (and https://thithucdientu.gov.vn — the
  immigration department's notice of 11 Nov 2024 moved the e-visa portal to these two
  domains; the old domain https://evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn now only redirects).
  Checked 2026-10-07.
- Fee: US$25 / US$50 — stated on evisa.gov.vn and echoed by
  https://www.myvietnamvisa.com/vietnam-e-visa-fee.html (checked 2026-10-07). The
  $25/$50 figures are consistent across sources; I could not load a fee *page* on
  evisa.gov.vn itself (the site is a JS app and returned near-empty to a text extract),
  so treat the fee as **high-confidence but sourced via third parties**.
- Processing: commonly quoted as **3 working days** after submission and payment.
  Third-party (https://www.myvietnamvisa.com/vietnam-evisa-processing-time.html,
  checked 2026-10-07) — **not verified against the official portal**.

**Warning:** `vietnamvisa.govt.vn`, `vietnam-evisa.com`, `myvietnamvisa.com` etc. are
commercial intermediaries. They are not the government. The government portals are only
`evisa.gov.vn` and `thithucdientu.gov.vn`.

## Passport validity, blank pages, onward ticket

- **6 months' validity** beyond date of entry is the rule stated in Vietnam's own
  exemption list for Singapore ("valid periods of at least 6 months"). Renew first if
  the passport expires before roughly July 2027.
  Source: https://vietnam.gov.vn/visa-exemption-68946 (checked 2026-10-07)
- **Onward/return ticket:** the same official page makes it a condition of the
  Singapore exemption ("together with return air tickets or air tickets for leaving the
  country"). In practice airlines check this at SIN check-in more often than Vietnamese
  immigration does. **Have the return booking reference or a PDF on your phone.**
- Blank pages: no specific published requirement for Vietnam was found. Not confirmed.

## Visa-on-arrival vs e-visa
Not applicable to a Singapore passport holder — there is nothing to apply for. Vietnam's
"visa on arrival" is in any case a private-agent pre-approval letter scheme, not an
airport-issued visa, and should be avoided by anyone who *does* need a visa: use
`evisa.gov.vn` directly instead.

## Pre-arrival information declaration
HCMC's Tan Son Nhat airport now requires foreign nationals to submit personal information
**before arrival** (online at https://prearrival.immigration.gov.vn/), receiving a QR code
to show immigration. **Kester lands at Noi Bai (Hanoi), and MFA's page says this is
currently only effective at Tan Son Nhat.** Nothing to do for a Hanoi arrival — but check
again closer to departure in case it has been rolled out nationally.
Source: https://www.mfa.gov.sg/travelling-overseas/travel-advisories-notices-and-visa-information/vietnam/ (checked 2026-10-07)

## Customs

- **Duty-free allowance into Vietnam:** 1.5 L of liquor above 22% ABV *or* 2 L below 22%;
  400 cigarettes; 100 cigars; 500 g raw tobacco; other goods up to **VND 5,000,000** in
  value. Over that, declare and pay duty. Source: Vietnam Government Portal, Customs
  section, https://vietnam.gov.vn/visa-exemption-68946 (checked 2026-10-07) — **official**.
- **Medication (this is the real risk area):**
  - Prescription medicines should travel with the **doctor's prescription** and a supply
    roughly matching the trip. Commonly reported limits: 30 days for ordinary
    prescription drugs; **7 days for narcotic-class** medicines; **10 days for
    psychotropic / precursor-containing** medicines (Vietnamese Decree 54/2017 is the
    instrument usually cited).
  - Over-the-counter products that are harmless in Singapore can be controlled in
    Vietnam — the usual trap is **codeine** (many Singapore cough syrups and
    panadol-with-codeine products) and high-dose **pseudoephedrine**.
  - Sources: https://entryvn.com/bringing-medications/ and
    https://vietnamclinic.com/blog/customs-rules-prescription-medications-vietnam
    (both checked 2026-10-07). **Both are commercial sites — treat the 7/10/30-day
    figures as leads, not verified law.** I could not open an official Vietnamese
    customs page stating these limits. **Action: if anyone takes a regular prescription
    (especially anything opioid, sedative, ADHD or codeine-based), get it in writing
    from the doctor and check with the Vietnamese embassy in Singapore
    (https://vnembassy-singapore.mofa.gov.vn) before flying.**
  - Walk the **red channel** and declare if carrying controlled medicines.
- Cash declaration threshold: **not verified.** I could not source the current figure
  from an official Vietnamese customs page. If either of you carries more than
  ~US$5,000-equivalent in cash, declare it rather than risk a seizure.

## Health

CDC's Vietnam country page (https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/vietnam,
checked 2026-10-07) and the CDC Yellow Book Vietnam chapter
(https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/asia/vietnam.html, checked 2026-10-07):

| Vaccine | CDC position for this trip |
|---|---|
| Hepatitis A | Recommended for unvaccinated travellers |
| Hepatitis B | Recommended for unvaccinated travellers of all ages |
| Typhoid | Recommended for most travellers |
| Japanese encephalitis | **Consider** — relevant here: CDC says consider for short-term travellers *doing activities that increase risk: hiking, biking, rural areas, or staying without screens/AC/netting*. The Ha Giang loop (rural, homestays) sits squarely in that bucket. Endemic throughout Vietnam. |
| Rabies | **Consider** — pre-exposure for travellers doing outdoor/caving activities; CDC notes rabid dogs occur in Vietnam and post-exposure vaccine is generally available. Ha Giang homestays have dogs. |
| Routine (MMR, Tdap, polio, flu) | Be up to date |

- Malaria: CDC's Vietnam page does not make antimalarials a blanket recommendation for
  Hanoi/Ninh Binh/northern highlands; risk is low in the cities and in the cool dry
  season. **Not verified for Ha Giang specifically — take a doctor's advice.**
- Dengue: present in Vietnam, mosquito-borne, no vaccine routinely advised for a short
  trip. Use repellent. (CDC page, same URL, checked 2026-10-07.)
- Tap water is **not** drinkable — MFA says avoid unboiled water and ice from unknown
  sources: https://www.mfa.gov.sg/travelling-overseas/travel-advisories-notices-and-visa-information/vietnam/ (checked 2026-10-07)
- **Travel insurance:** MFA "strongly encourages" comprehensive travel insurance beyond
  the Vietnamese government's minimum. This matters more than usual here — see the
  motorbike section of `transport.md`: **most travel policies will not cover a
  foreigner riding a motorbike in Vietnam without a valid Vietnamese-recognised
  licence**, and Kester's Singapore IDP is not recognised. Check the policy wording.
  Emergency numbers: police 113, fire 114, ambulance 115.

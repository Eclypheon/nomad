# Transport — SIN↔Hanoi, getting around, and the motorbike question

Researched 2026-10-07. Fares marked "typical" are planning ranges from 2026 sources;
nothing here was quoted live for 24 Dec 2026 – 2 Jan 2027 unless stated.

## 1. Flights: Singapore (SIN) → Hanoi (HAN)

**Non-stop carriers:** **Scoot (TR)**, **VietJet Air (VJ)**, **Vietnam Airlines (VN)** and
**Singapore Airlines (SQ)**. Flight time **~3 h 15 m – 3 h 30 m**. Two to three daily
departures each on the LCCs; SQ runs roughly 2 daily. Source for the carrier set and the
fare bands: ShopBack SG, "Cheapest Month to Fly Singapore to Hanoi in 2026"
(https://www.shopback.sg/blog/travel/sg-cheapest-month-fly-singapore-to-hanoi-2026,
checked 2026-10-07).

**Typical return fares (SGD), shoulder season — NOT verified for the holiday window:**

| Carrier | Type | Typical return band | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scoot (TR) | Low-cost | ~S$220–480 | Checked bags are extra (roughly US$25–40 each way) |
| VietJet (VJ) | Low-cost | ~S$230–500 | Overnight departure option |
| Vietnam Airlines (VN) | Full-service | (not in the ShopBack table) | Non-stop, bags + meal included |
| Singapore Airlines (SQ) | Full-service | ~S$380–780 | 2 daily, bags + meal |

- Scoot's own site was displaying **"from SGD 226"** for SIN–HAN with an October-2026 fare
  calendar showing SIN–HAN "from SGD 500" on the expensive dates
  (https://www.flyscoot.com/flights/en/flights-from-singapore-to-hanoi, checked 2026-10-07).
- Aggregators quote one-way from US$108–118 and round-trip from US$187, with an average
  round trip around US$437 (Expedia, Trip.com route pages, checked 2026-10-07 — these
  numbers are **all-dates averages and teaser "from" prices; treat them as noise**).

**⚠️ Could not confirm:** I did not obtain a live fare quote for 24 Dec 2026 → 1/2 Jan
2027. Doing that requires a booking-engine session and the brief forbids booking. **Expect
this window to price above every band above.** 24 Dec – 2 Jan is the single most expensive
week of the year on this route (Christmas out, New Year back, and it's also peak season in
Hanoi). **Budget S$450–700 return per person as a planning number and verify before you
lock the trip's dates to it.** If the fare comes in high, shifting departure to 26/27 Dec
and return to 3/4 Jan is usually materially cheaper — but that eats trip days.

**Non-stop vs one-stop:** one-stop routings via Bangkok/KL/Saigon save little and cost
4–8 hours; for a 3h20m route, non-stop is the correct call. Not worth researching further.

**Da Nang / Saigon instead?** No. Da Nang is ~800 km south of Hanoi — for a *northern*
trip it adds a domestic flight or a 15+ hour train. Saigon is further still. The only
reason to fly into either would be a multi-region trip, which this isn't.

## 5. Hanoi → Lao Cai / Sapa (overnight train) — covered because the brief asks

**Not recommended for this trip** (Sapa is excluded, see `weather.md`), but the facts:
- The **SP1/SP2/SP3/SP4** overnight trains run nightly, Hanoi (~21:35) → Lao Cai (~05:00),
  then ~1 h by road up to Sapa. There are **no trains to Ha Giang.**
- Classes on the SP trains: **4-berth soft sleeper** (the standard tourist choice),
  **6-berth hard sleeper**, and soft seats. Vietnamese Railways (DSVN) soft sleepers are
  the honest choice; privately-run "tourist" sleeping cars (e.g. Violette) cost roughly
  **twice** the DSVN price for an identical compartment with nicer bedding.
- Fares: aggregators list Hanoi–Lao Cai from **~490,000 VND** for a seat, with soft
  sleepers substantially more (https://vexere.com/vn/trains/from-hanoi-to-lao-cai-lao-cai.24.480.en,
  checked 2026-10-07 — **aggregator**). Seat61 has the timetable and vehicle details
  (https://www.seat61.com/Vietnam.htm, checked 2026-10-07).
- **Verdict:** fine for Sapa, irrelevant for Ha Giang. Don't build the trip around it.

## 6. 🚨 MOTORBIKING — the legal trap (get this right)

### What the law says
- Vietnam **acceded to the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic (in force for Vietnam
  since 2015)** and recognises an IDP issued under **that** convention, **used together
  with the matching national licence**, within the categories it covers.
  Sources: https://vietnamlawmagazine.vn/how-can-foreigners-obtain-international-driving-permits-in-vietnam-75857.html
  (Vietnam Law Magazine, checked 2026-10-07) and
  https://rentabikevn.com/international-driving-permit-the-ultimate-guide/ (checked
  2026-10-07 — **operator/rental company, but the claims match the Vietnamese-language
  legal coverage**).
- **A 1949 Geneva Convention IDP is the wrong document.** "Vietnam's statutory
  foreign-IDP route is based on the 1968 Convention, so a perfectly real 1949 permit can
  still be the wrong document for riding here." Same source, checked 2026-10-07.
- **A car licence never covers a motorbike.** Anything above 50cc requires a motorcycle
  entitlement on the licence (Vietnam's own classes; A1 covers the smaller bikes).
  Source: https://daytripsvietnam.com/guides/driving-in-vietnam-idp-rules-2026/ (checked
  2026-10-07 — **commercial guide**).
- The **US Embassy in Hanoi** goes further and states plainly: *"Foreign driver licenses,
  even when accompanied by an international driving permit, are not valid in Vietnam."*
  https://vn.usembassy.gov/driving-in-vietnam/ (checked 2026-10-07). **This conflicts
  with the 1968-IDP position above.** Both are cited by serious people; do not assume the
  permissive reading is the one a traffic policeman will take.

### 🚨 What applies to Kester specifically (Singapore licence holder)
**AA Singapore issues IDPs under the 1949 Convention, and AA Singapore says so on its own
site — including who doesn't accept it.**

> "Following the Convention Road Traffic of 19 September 1949, this international driving
> licence issued by AA Singapore is valid for 1-year from the date of issue. … This
> driving permit is valid in the territory of all the Contracting States of Convention on
> Road Traffic of 19 September 1949. **Please note that China, Myanmar and Vietnam do not
> recognise this international driving licence.**"
> — Automobile Association of Singapore, https://aas.com.sg/idp-carnet/ (checked 2026-10-07)

**This is the primary-source answer to the trap the brief flagged.** A Singapore IDP is
a 1949 permit; Vietnam only recognises 1968 permits; therefore **a Singapore licence +
Singapore IDP does not legally qualify Kester to ride a >50cc motorbike in Vietnam.** This
is also exactly what travel forums report
(https://www.facebook.com/groups/456831184922110/posts/1648631655742051/ — **social, lead only**).

### Consequence: insurance
No insurer will pay a claim for an accident while riding unlicensed. Treat "does travel
insurance cover motorbike riding in Vietnam?" as **no, not for a rider without a
Vietnamese-recognised licence.** I did not open specific policy wordings to quote them —
**this is a reasoned conclusion from the licensing position, not a quoted policy term.**
**Ask the insurer in writing and keep the reply.**

### The 50cc loophole (and why I won't recommend it)
It is widely repeated that ≤50cc bikes need no licence in Vietnam and that this covers
foreigners. **I could not verify this from any official source**, and a 50cc scooter is
the wrong tool for the Ma Pi Leng pass anyway. Treat as unverified; do not build the trip
on it.

### → Therefore: do the Ha Giang loop as an EASY RIDER, not self-ride
Easy rider = a local licensed driver on his own bike, you as pillion. It:
- removes the licence problem entirely (the driver is the one who must be licensed),
- removes the mountain-riding risk (Ma Pi Leng has no guardrails and local traffic),
- costs little more than self-ride, and
- is what the overwhelming majority of visitors do, including experienced riders.
Worth stating honestly: **this is a real change to the "motorbiking" half of your brief** —
you'll be on the back of a bike for three days, not in control of it. It is still
three days of mountain riding, and it is the only version that is legal and insurable.

### 🚩 Border-area permit — IN FLUX, RE-CHECK BEFORE YOU GO
- Vietnam has **long** required foreigners to have permission to enter certain land-border
  districts, and the loop's core — **Quan Ba, Yen Minh, Dong Van, Meo Vac** — is inside
  that zone.
- From **1 June 2026** some operators began advising that a **Border Area Entry Permit**
  (~US$10, commonly 200,000–250,000 VND; one operator quotes 400,000 VND) is now being
  checked at active checkpoints along the whole loop, for tour bookers and solo riders
  alike. Sources: https://www.timeout.com/asia/news/ha-giang-loop-permits-what-travellers-need-to-know-before-riding-vietnams-famous-mountain-route-061426
  (Time Out, 14 June 2026) and https://hagiangtrips.com/pages/ha-giang-border-permit
  (operator).
- **Time Out's own conclusion is the important part:** *"this does not currently appear to
  be backed by an official government notice … it is safest to treat the June 1 date as
  operator guidance, not confirmed law."* Checked 2026-10-07.
- **Practical action:** ask your operator **in writing** whether the border permit is
  included and who pays it. Carry your passport and cash on the loop. Most operators
  include it; independent riders apply at local immigration/police in Ha Giang city.

## 8. Grab / ride-hailing outside Hanoi
- **Hanoi:** Grab and Xanh SM (the local EV taxi brand) both work well. MFA names
  **Xanh SM, ABC, Be, Mai Linh and CP** as the reputable Hanoi taxi companies.
  https://www.mfa.gov.sg/travelling-overseas/travel-advisories-notices-and-visa-information/vietnam/ (checked 2026-10-07)
- **Ninh Binh:** Grab works in Ninh Binh city and around Tam Coc; coverage is thinner than
  Hanoi but usable. Taxis wait at the railway station. Hotels arrange cars. **Grab in the
  Tam Coc/Trang An countryside can be slow to find — your homestay is the more reliable
  dispatcher.**
- **Ha Giang:** **treat Grab as unreliable.** Ha Giang city has some coverage; on the loop
  there is effectively none, and drivers commonly ask to be paid in cash for the short
  bus-station-to-hotel hop (~30,000–50,000 VND). **Not independently verified for Ha Giang
  — flagging this as an inference from the region's infrastructure, not a sourced claim.**
- **Set up Grab before you fly** — the app may want a local number or payment method.
  (vietpeep, checked 2026-10-07.)
